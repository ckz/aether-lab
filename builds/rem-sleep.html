REM SLEEP

a late-night listening companion
Twelve tracks for the space between waking and dreaming — when the mind untethers, memories dissolve into music, and the ceiling becomes a sky. Play loud enough to fill the room, soft enough to hide in.

current hour, wherever you are
01
Weightless
Marconi Union
Engineered with sound therapists to slow heart rate and reduce anxiety. If one song could medically induce REM, this is it. Eight minutes of weightlessness. Play it first.
8:09
02
Gymnopédie No. 1
Erik Satie
Composed in 1888, sounds like it was recorded in a dream in 1988. Satie called them "furniture music" — background for living. But at 2 AM they become foreground. Each repetition rewrites the room.
3:05
03
Avril 14th
Aphex Twin
A piano piece from the electronic wizard. Disarming in its simplicity — like finding a handwritten note in a machine. The slight out-of-tune-ness makes it feel human, or half-remembered.
2:00
04
An Ending, Ascent
Brian Eno
From "Apollo: Atmospheres & Soundtracks." Eno made this for a documentary about the moon landing that never got made. So it exists in the space between intention and reality — perfect for 3 AM.
4:16
05
Holocene
Bon Iver
"And at once I knew I was not magnificent." The most honest sentence in modern music, delivered over fingerpicking that sounds like snow falling in a canyon. Feels like looking through a window at something vast and quietly accepting.
5:37
06
Dragging a Living Desk
Nujabes
Lo-fi hip-hop from the producer who understood melancholy as warmth rather than cold. Nujabes died at 36 in a car accident. His music is a memorial to gentleness. Feels like rain on glass in a city you've never visited.
4:08
07
On the Nature of Daylight
Max Richter
Written over a single 48-hour period during a depressive episode. Richter looped the same phrase until it became something larger than grief — something luminous. The strings swell like a lung inhaling. Used in films about time running out.
6:20
08
Where Is My Mind?
Maxence Cyrin
Pixies' song, but played solo on piano by a French classical musician who understood both traditions. Stripped of feedback and urgency, the melody reveals itself as genuinely haunting. Like finding the skeleton of a song you loved.
2:57
09
Everything In Its Right Place
Radiohead
Recorded in one take. Thom Yorke's voice was "completely exhausted." The F# arpeggio repeats for six minutes like a mantra — and by the end, the exhaustion becomes transcendent. The sound of accepting chaos and finding peace inside it.
4:11
10
Sæglópur
Sigur Rós
"Sæglópur" means "lost at sea" in Icelandic. The song opens with a guitar bowed like a cello and builds into a choir of voices singing in a language no one speaks. Post-rock as emotional cartography — maps of feelings that don't have names.
7:36
11
Spiegel im Spiegel
Arvo Pärt
"Mirror in the mirror." The simplest piece on this list — piano and violin, each note breathing in silence. Pärt's tintinnabuli style treats each note like a bell that keeps ringing after being struck. Play this when words feel like too much.
6:22
12
1/1
Brian Eno
The final track from "Music for Airports" — the piece that invented ambient music. Four tape loops of different lengths, never repeating the same way twice. Eno designed it so you could leave it playing forever and never notice. The perfect ending. You are not magnificent. You are here. That is enough.
